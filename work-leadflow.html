LeadFlow

A landing page for a CRM built around one promise: capture leads faster, without the clutter.

Role
UI/UX Design
Timeline
Early 2026

The brief

LeadFlow needed a marketing site that could hold its own against category leaders in a crowded CRM market, without burying the pitch under jargon. The brief was simple to say and harder to design for: make “everything you need, nothing you don’t” something a visitor could feel in the first five seconds, not just read as a tagline.

Say it once, clearly

The hero leads with the promise, not the product, using “All your leads. One organized platform.” as the headline, paired with a single supporting line and two calls to action: one committed, one exploratory. A dark-to-light gradient pulls the eye down toward a real product photo, and trust logos sit above the fold instead of being pushed down to a footer afterthought.

A grid that isn’t a checklist

Most SaaS feature sections repeat the same card four times over. Here, an asymmetric layout (one full-width automation panel, then a mixed grid where a single dark card pulls focus toward the platform’s strongest feature) breaks that rhythm and gives the eye somewhere to land first, instead of scanning past four identical boxes.

Close on trust, then get out of the way

Three testimonials in a plain three-up grid, a closing call to action on a dark gradient band, and a footer that does its job without competing for attention. The page ends the way it started: direct, and unafraid of a little empty space.
