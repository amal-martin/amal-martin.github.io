AlumniConnect

An alumni network and management platform for Kristu Jayanti: a public site, a member experience, and a full admin backend, designed end to end.

Role
UI/UX Design

The brief

Kristu Jayanti’s alumni network existed mostly informally: batch WhatsApp groups, the occasional reunion email, no single place to find someone or hear about an opportunity. AlumniConnect needed to give thousands of graduates one home: somewhere to find each other, hire each other, and keep showing up for the university that shaped them.

One good first impression

The public site leads with the promise, not a login form, using “Where Alumni Connect, Collaborate, and Grow” as the headline, then gets straight to what the network actually does: a private hiring pool, alumni-founded startups, and industry circles, each carrying its own accent color so the feature grid doesn’t read as one long list.

A home base, not a homepage

Once inside, the experience turns personal, with a first-name greeting, live network stats, and a feed built around what’s actually relevant: recommended jobs, upcoming events nearby, and what your own batch has been posting. Quick access and recent activity sit in a fixed sidebar, so returning members never have to hunt for the things they check most.

Finding someone shouldn’t take five clicks

The directory leans on filter chips over dropdown menus, and gives every card just enough (name, batch, current role, one line of context) before a visitor has to commit to opening a full profile.

The profile as a career story

A profile isn’t just a bio. It’s a timeline. Career history reads chronologically, top to bottom, with community-impact numbers like mentees guided and events attended sitting alongside it, quietly rewarding the alumni who stay involved, not just the ones who show up once.

Built to be run, not just used

Behind the member-facing app is a full admin panel: batch-wise breakdowns, industry distribution, network growth at a glance. A platform like this lives or dies by whether the people running it can keep it healthy, so the admin dashboard got the same design attention as the public pages, not an afterthought.

Moderation that doesn’t feel like a chore

Every job and startup listing alumni post goes through a lightweight approval queue: clear pending, approved, and rejected counts up top, one-click approve or reject per card. A small detail, but it’s the difference between an admin panel people dread opening and one they actually use daily.
